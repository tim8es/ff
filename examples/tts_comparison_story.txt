Every night at eleven, the little town library turned off its lights. Except for one window on the top floor. For forty years, that window glowed until sunrise.

People invented explanations. A secret laboratory. A ghost. Even a treasure hidden behind the books.

Children would count how long it stayed bright, hoping the light might blink in response. During thunderstorms, they watched from across the street, convinced the lonely window was waiting for somebody.

Then one winter, the building was renovated. A worker climbed the stairs and opened a dusty door. Inside was a desk lamp connected to an ancient timer. Beside it lay a notebook.

Every page held a date, a name, and the same six words: You are not forgotten. Not tonight.

The librarian had started the ritual after the town lost its first volunteer firefighter. Every evening, that single light meant somebody was remembered.

The worker replaced the timer. And when the library reopened, the window still shone.
